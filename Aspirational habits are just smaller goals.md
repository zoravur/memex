We think of goals and habits as different things, but I think collapsing that distinction is beneficial to achievement.

First, let's talk about the differences. Colloquially, habits are small activities repeated frequently, whereas a goal is usually the result of a lot of smaller activities. While a goal is a state you want to be in, a habit names a smaller action. However, a habit can easily be transformed into a goal: "I want to be the type of person that does action X consistently". Conversely, a goal can be turned into a habit: "I will repeatedly do \[what it takes, i.e. collection of habits\] to achieve goal Y". In short, goals and habits differ in 1. granularity and 2. whether it is a state or an action, grammatically.

Whether we see something as a goal or a habit fundamentally boils down to whether we are going from 0->1 or 1->N. In the former, we're achieving a goal. In the latter, we are building a habit. We think of 0->1 as atomic, but that just means we're focusing on the subproblems. Take, for instance, a student that gets poor grades because they don't know how to study. Studying is a skill that consists of many sub-skills, such as note-taking, time management, self-testing and self-understanding. The poor student must individually learn all of these skills before they can say they "know how to study", and then they have to apply it to one test to get a single A, and repeatedly to get many As. Whereas for the straight-A student, all they have to do is think "I'm going to study for this test", and then they do it. For them, the action of studying is atomic.

In the former scenario, we viewed "studying" both as a goal and as a habit. This implies that whether or not something is a goal or a habit depends on skill. Many people view weight loss as a goal. When the skill of weight loss becomes automatic, such as for bodybuilders, it's called "cutting weight".

For me, once I collapsed the distinction between habits and goals, I was psychologically much more willing to approach things that I found difficult at a more gradual pace, and therefore had much more success. Understanding that other people's habits might just be your goals gives you license to expend effort, even when you feel like things should be easier than they are. And once you're in a feedback loop where you're consistently exceeding your own realistic expectations, your skill will compound much more rapidly.
